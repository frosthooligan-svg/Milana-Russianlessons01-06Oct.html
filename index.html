04 · Grammar focus

Articles with geographical names

The brochure includes a compact reference to articles. Apply the rule while describing places.

Usually no “the”

Continents: Asia, Europe

Most countries: Russia, France, Japan

Cities: Moscow, Kazan

Lakes: Lake Baikal, Lake Ladoga

Usually use “the”

Rivers: the Volga, the Lena

Seas and oceans: the Black Sea, the Pacific Ocean

Mountain ranges: the Ural Mountains, the Caucasus Mountains

Some country names: the United States

Remember: say Lake Baikal, but the deepest lake in the world. The first is a name; the second is a descriptive phrase.

06 · Reading

A country of contrasts

Russia is the largest country in the world, and its territory stretches across Eastern Europe and Northern Asia. Because of its size, the country contains a wide variety of landscapes and natural environments. These include forests, mountains, rivers, lakes and coastal areas. Russia also has significant natural resources, which play an important role in its economy.

Its cities differ in history, architecture and atmosphere. Moscow is the capital and an important political and cultural centre. Saint Petersburg is famous for its historic architecture and museums. Other cities are known for industry, science, transport or their natural surroundings. As a result, travelling around Russia can reveal very different sides of the same country.

However, describing a country involves more than listing facts. A useful description connects details to a wider idea. For example, Russia's geographical size helps explain its regional diversity, while its history has influenced the architecture and traditions found in different places. To understand a country, it is useful to consider both its physical geography and the people who live there.
